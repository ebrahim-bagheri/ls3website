## Before you write to us

Applications to work in the lab go through the University of Toronto's central admissions
process — being in touch with us is not a substitute for applying, and we cannot admit anyone
outside it. What contact does do is make sure your application is read by someone who is
looking for it.

Read a couple of our recent papers first. The [research themes](/research/) are the fastest way
in, and each one lists the work that defines it. An email that engages with something specific
we have written will get a reply; a mass-mailed one almost certainly will not.

## What to send

Email Professor Bagheri directly, with:

- A short summary of your background and research experience.
- Why you think you are a good match for the work being done at LS3 — name the theme or the paper.
- Your CV, and a transcript if you have one to hand.

Use a subject line beginning `[LS3 application]` so your message is filtered correctly and not lost.

## Positions

### PhD students

Apply to a doctoral programme at the Faculty of Information. Funding for admitted doctoral
students is arranged through the Faculty; we will discuss lab-specific support during the process.

### Master's students

Master's students join the lab for thesis or project work. If you are already enrolled at the
University of Toronto and looking for a supervisor, say so — the route is different and usually
faster.

### Postdoctoral researchers

Postdoctoral positions open with funded projects rather than on a fixed calendar. Write with a
CV, a research statement, and two or three representative papers.

### Undergraduate and visiting researchers

We take a small number of undergraduate researchers each term, usually through University of
Toronto research programmes. Visiting doctoral students with their own funding are welcome to
enquire; the lab has hosted visitors from Brazil, France, Iran and Canada.

## What working here is like

The lab publishes at SIGIR, CIKM, ECIR, WSDM, TheWebConf, EMNLP and ICWSM, and in journals
including TOIS, TIST and Information Processing & Management. Students lead their own papers —
look at the author lists on the [publications page](/publications/) and you will see students in
first position on most of them.

Projects are collaborative. Most papers have three or more lab authors, and much of the work sits
across the boundary between retrieval methods and their social consequences, so we read widely
and argue a lot.
